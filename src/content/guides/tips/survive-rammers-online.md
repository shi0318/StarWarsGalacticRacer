## The online problem players are talking about
A month after launch, the busiest conversations on Galactic Racer's Steam forums are about other people. On November 4, 2026, one of the most active General Discussions threads was "Any good racing strategies?", which opened with a familiar complaint: being overtaken and rammed, taking the wrong paths, and crashing into the same pillar on a shortcut. Close by, a "Cheater plague" thread had passed 20 replies and a "When are they adding ranked mode?" thread had 11. The common thread is that online Tours feel more violent than the campaign, and that some players cannot tell whether they are losing to skill, shortcuts, or something unfair.

This guide gathers the defensive advice that experienced players have been sharing, checks it against what the game confirms through its achievements and patch notes, and is clear about what is not confirmed. It complements our <a href="/tips/shunt-takedown/">shunt and takedown guide</a>, which is about hitting other racers. This page is about surviving them.

## Start with Resilience, not top speed
The most detailed reply in the November 4 strategy thread made a simple point: human players ram more aggressively than the AI pilots, so you need to level Resilience to take hits from rams and weapons. Another reply in the same thread put it more bluntly, saying speed is not everything and suggesting defensive parts such as a shield and autopilot.

That matches long-standing advice on this site. Our <a href="/tips/multiplayer-tour/">multiplayer Tour guide</a> already lists Resilience before the acid planet as a priority, and <a href="/tips/wreck-out-recovery/">wreck-out recovery</a> explains why resilience comes before speed in the shop. The reason is practical. A build that wins a clean race but explodes after two shunts will not finish a six-race Tour against people actively hunting contact. If you are regularly knocked out mid-pack, buy survivability first and add speed once you are reliably finishing.

## Shields are the counter players name most
Shields came up more than any other ability. One player in the strategy thread said to always take a shield if you can, or to play as Nik, because without one you will be crashed constantly by rammers. Nik Skandaro is listed with the Survivor Racer Style in third-party multiplayer guides; see our <a href="/tips/racer-styles/">Racer Styles guide</a> for how styles differ.

The longest reply called Shields "vital" for surviving attacks and named two upgrade paths players have seen in the game. Surging Shield was suggested if you want to open up your ability set, while Impact Shield was preferred for aggressive ramming with an increased effect. Timing matters as much as choice. In a separate thread about Sebulba events, a player advised picking up a shield, waiting for the screen to say you are being targeted, and only then using it, while also watching for mines. A shield fired early is a shield you do not have when the shot arrives.

## Keep Surge going and your Energy topped up
The same strategy reply said you need some way of getting Surging, describing it as a status that boosts engine power across the board, increasing speed and acceleration. If your Racer Style does not start with it, the advice was to pick up the Surge ability. The game confirms the status matters enough to track: the Steam achievement **Speed is Dangerous, Embrace it Anyway** asks you to gain Surge for 60 seconds continuously, and Valve's global stats showed 12.0% of players had it on November 4, 2026.

For keeping several abilities available, that player recommended the Status Scrubber, upgraded into the Multi-Scrubber, to keep Energy reserves topped up. They also suggested combining Impact Shield with Impact Surge, so that slamming into rivals keeps Surge running as long as there are racers ahead to target. That is an aggressive loop, so only use it if your Resilience can absorb the return hits. The achievement **Four Arms Would Be Useful**, for equipping four Abilities at once, was held by 20.9% of players on the same date, so a full ability bar is still something most players have not run.

## Pick contact abilities that suit your vehicle
Vehicle shape matters for close combat. The strategy reply recommended Flame Vents specifically on the Landspeeder, explaining that the other vehicle types, podracers included, tilt when turning, which makes Flame or Corrosive Vents less likely to hit rivals. For other vehicles, the suggestion was Vent II with either Supercooled Vents or Auto-Vent. If you prefer the Landspeeder, our <a href="/vehicles/landspeeders/">Landspeeder guide</a> covers its handling.

For close-range fights, the player liked Shockwave, which pushes nearby racers around and leaves them open to being rammed into obstacles. They described Vectored Shockwave as the choice if you need more speed and Magnetic Shockwave if you mainly want to deal damage. They were less keen on Mines and noted that Ion Bolts become useless once you get ahead, which matters if you plan to lead from the front. The achievement **Speed and Violence**, which requires hitting five racers with Ion Bolt in one Standard or Eliminator race using the Tech Expert style, was earned by just 1.2% of players on November 4, so do not build an online plan around landing lots of bolts. **Raging Rancor**, taking down five racers in one race with the Brawler style, sat at 10.9%.

## Use your boosts to escape, not just to pass
Getting away from a pack is a defensive move. The strategy reply explained that Afterburners help you get up to speed and add a modest increase to maximum speed, while Ramjets give a power boost that depends on how fast you are already going. Their advice was to use Afterburners after a series of technical corners and Ramjets once your speed is up. A November 3 Steam review from a player with over 50 hours added two related tips: online races have a catch-up speed boost for last place after a crash, and pushing forward to tilt your racer at very high speed does not reduce speed but improves your view. Both are player observations, not documented rules, but they are easy to test in a single race.

If a heavy hit puts you at the back, do not fight the player who just rammed you. Use the recovery boost, stay out of the pack, and rebuild your position on clean straights. Our <a href="/tips/ramjet-heat/">Ramjet heat guide</a> explains how to avoid overheating while chasing.

## Learn the tracks before you blame other players
The cheater thread showed real frustration, but replies were mixed. Some players said they had not met an obvious cheater. One reply noted that a few shortcuts make a huge difference, which can make a rival seem impossibly fast. Another said that betting on a podium and achieving it lets you buy strong parts. Others blamed a balance issue around one multiplayer racer, Lyren Shok, rather than cheating. None of this is confirmed by Fuse Games: as of November 4, 2026, no Steam news post or patch note mentions cheating, anti-cheat, or racer balance changes.

The most practical fix is the one a reply in the strategy thread summed up as "knowledge is king": earn Gold on every Arcade challenge to learn tracks, builds, and vehicles. Valve's stats show 14.1% of players have beaten any Arcade Gold time and 0.5% have beaten 50. Our <a href="/tips/galactic-racer-arcade-practice-guide/">Arcade practice guide</a> covers how to get started.

## What is and is not in the game right now
There is no ranked mode or skill-based ladder, and Fuse Games has not announced one. A player in the ranked thread argued that a ranking system would help weaker players avoid lobbies dominated by veterans; that is a request, not a plan. The confirmed recent change is the October 22 quality-of-life update, which raised online party size from three to six, raised credits for joining a Tour mid-way from 4,000 to 6,000, and added party stability fixes. Private lobbies are still missing; see <a href="/tips/no-private-lobbies/">no private lobbies</a>. For playing with friends, read our <a href="/six-player-tour-race-plan/">six-player Tour plan</a>.

Until the multiplayer changes, the plan is straightforward. Put Resilience first, take a shield and save it for the moment you are targeted, keep Surge active, choose contact abilities that suit your vehicle, and learn the shortcuts that make rivals look like cheaters.
