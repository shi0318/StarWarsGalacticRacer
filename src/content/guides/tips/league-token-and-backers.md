After qualifying for the Galactic League in the single-player campaign, choose a Backer by the contract you can complete, not by the character's name. Backers provide the League Token needed to take part in a Tour. Their contracts support the resources you receive during it. A rivalry can also put a Token at stake, so understand the contract before adding that separate risk.

![A podracer in a Galactic League stadium](/images/guide/stadium-pod.webp)

## What the Backer decision actually establishes

The official Rivals and Backers explanation names Vranki, Sidon Ithano and Q-33N. It says contracts keep the Backer happy and the resources coming. It does not publish a universal payout table, a ranking of the three Backers or the exact conditions of every offer you may see.

Use the offer displayed in your campaign as the decision record. Read the requested action, any target or restriction, and the consequences the interface describes. If a condition is unclear, do not replace it with a remembered sentence from a different contract. A familiar name can accompany a task that requires a different preparation from the one you expected.

The Token's role is Tour entry. Do not confuse that with a cosmetic purchase, ordinary upgrade spending or a rivalry stake. Keeping those jobs separate makes it easier to understand why a decision matters. If progression seems blocked, identify the objective currently displayed before assuming that another ordinary race will award the missing requirement.

## Compare the action, the route and the craft

Before confirming an offer, ask three questions. Can you perform the requested action deliberately? Can you identify a suitable opportunity in the event you expect to drive? Does the selected craft let you repeat it without losing control? A contract can look appealing and still be a poor match for what you are prepared to execute.

Vranki's example in the official explanation involves jumps for the crowd. That is useful information about the type of task, but not a complete scoring specification. For Sidon Ithano and Q-33N, read the actual offers; this guide does not assign them invented specialties or guaranteed rewards.

| Decision | Evidence to use |
| --- | --- |
| Is this the right Backer? | The current contract's action and restrictions |
| Can I execute it? | A deliberate practice attempt with a suitable craft |
| Did the action count? | The objective feedback and result shown by the game |
| Should I add a rivalry? | The displayed stake and your preparation for that opponent |

If all three offers are unfamiliar, choose from the conditions you can understand rather than guessing from the names. A contract is a commitment to a task; its appeal should come from knowing how to attempt it.

## Prepare the maneuver before the stakes

Use an appropriate practice event when one is available. Practice the action itself, including its recovery. Landing a jump while missing the next turn is not yet a reliable routine. A successful contact maneuver that sends your craft into the boundary has the same problem.

Keep the craft and control layout familiar while learning the contract action. If you change both at once, you introduce uncertainty that the contract did not require. Read the [class guide](/vehicles/landspeeders/) for handling differences and the [Arcade guide](/tips/galactic-racer-arcade-practice-guide/) for focused practice.

For Vranki's jump example, use the [jump-contract plan](/tips/vranki-jump-contract/). It deals specifically with choosing a visible approach, preserving landing room and checking the objective afterward. Those execution details belong there; this page is about selecting and managing the Backer commitment.

## Keep the contract visible in your race plan

Before entering the event, state the contract goal in a short sentence. Then identify where it might conflict with another aim. An overtake may hide the approach you need to read. An aggressive rivalry may consume the space you need for the contract maneuver. If both cannot be attempted safely at once, decide which objective governs your next choice.

Do not assume that any visually impressive action counts. The current objective and its feedback determine that. If a jump or maneuver appears not to register, preserve the event name, contract wording and result. That information is more useful than repeating an entire Tour while changing several things.

Avoid asserting a hidden failure rule. A crash, a poor finish or a missed action may have consequences, but the precise condition must come from the game. Review the displayed outcome instead of assuming that a dramatic landing either completed or invalidated the contract.

## Add rivalry stakes as a separate decision

The official explanation warns that a rivalry can put a precious League Token on the line. It recommends getting to know the other pilot's racing style first. That is a reason to pause, not a command to accept every rivalry as soon as it appears.

Read the stake and compare it with your ability to complete the current Tour and contract. Use the [rivalry observation guide](/tips/choose-your-rival/) to prepare for the opponent. Do not treat a famous character or one successful shunt as evidence that the wager is affordable.

If you lose a stake, inspect the current progression state before deciding what comes next. This guide does not promise an immediate replacement Token, a recovery exploit or a fixed sequence of menus. Follow the requirement the campaign now displays.

## Review resources without inventing a payout formula

After the event, compare the contract feedback with the resources actually awarded. Note the event and the conditions, especially if you want to compare another offer on a later run. One result does not prove that a Backer always pays more or that a particular action has a hidden multiplier.

When choosing the next upgrade, address the problem that most threatens the next objective. A part can be attractive without helping the contract you have. If the craft is behaving abnormally, inspect its displayed condition and use the [Faulty Parts guide](/tips/faulty-parts/) before assuming the Backer changed its handling.

The useful order is qualification, contract reading, preparation, execution and result review. Keep each decision tied to the campaign information you can see. That produces a plan you can adjust when the next run offers a different combination of events and rivals.
