Sitting in another pilot's wake is the cheapest speed in the game. It is also, on at least one circuit, the reason people lose races they were leading — because the wake blinds you, and the hazard you cannot see is the one that ends the lap.

## How to use a draft

1. **Get in early on the straight,** not halfway along it. The benefit builds; it is not instant.
2. **Leave before the braking marker.** A draft that is still attached at turn-in means you are carrying the rival's line, not your own.
3. **Pull out to the inside,** so the exit belongs to you. Pulling out to the outside hands the corner back.
4. **Then spend the boost.** The afterburner out of the slipstream is the pass. The ramjet usually is not — see the [boost rules](/tips/ramjet-heat/).

## Draft blindness on Crait

The Crait salt flats are bright and open, and the published hazard for the circuit is exactly this: sitting in another pilot's draft blinds you. The lap is about choosing when to leave the wake, not how long you can hold it.

The same problem in weaker form shows up anywhere with dust or low visibility — Jakku's dust hides the racing line whether or not you are drafting, and Sentinel One is dark enough that losing a few metres of sight costs you the acid boundary.

## Draft, then shunt — in that order

A draft puts you alongside. It does not move anybody. The contact comes after:

- Use the slipstream to arrive, then **shunt on the straight and finish it before the apex**.
- Do not start contact from inside the wake. You cannot see the exit you need, and a shunt without an exit is a trade you lose.
- If the takedown animation points you at a barrier, reset the line before the next rival rather than chaining a second hit.

The full sequence is on the [shunt and takedown page](/tips/shunt-takedown/).

## Style note

**Slipstreamer** is one of the named racer styles. A style changes which events, bets and favours you are fishing for over a tour — it does not change the physics of the wake, and it does not make drafting into a corner safe.
