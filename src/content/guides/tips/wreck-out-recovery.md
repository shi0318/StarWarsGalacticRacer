Finishing fourth does not end a campaign tour. Crashing out too many times does. Once you understand that the tour has a wreck budget rather than a points budget, almost every build and racing decision changes.

## Spend the wreck budget on purpose

| Race state | What it costs | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Clean lap, poor position | Almost nothing | Finish it. Learn the braking markers |
| One hazard contact | Resilience | Back off the next corner and rebuild the line |
| Repeated contact in the pack | The tour | Drop out of the fight and take the position you have |
| Wreck-out | A life from the budget | Change the build before the next race, not the driving style mid-race |

That is why **resilience comes before speed** in the shop. Acid pools on Sentinel One and lava caves on Lantaana do not care about your lap time, and a multiplayer tour is six races of other people shunting you toward them.

## The three recoveries

**Mid-race.** Stop forcing contact. A rival who shunted you successfully is going to try again, and the second trade is always worse than the first because your resilience is already spent. Take the clean line, finish, bank the credits.

**Between races in a tour.** Spend the Hibi visit on the part that fixes your repeated mistake, not the part that sounds fastest. If you keep clipping walls, that is control. If you keep getting shunted off line, that is resilience. Livery and paint are free to look at and useless in a pool of acid.

**After a wreck-out.** Change the class before you change the driving. A landspeeder on Sentinel One finishes races that a speeder bike does not, and nothing about your lines has to improve for that to be true.

## Where tours actually end

Derven Acos is the League's custom arena and the finale, and its published hazard is that it stacks the conditions of the whole tour into one gauntlet. A build that was fine on exactly one planet fails there. If your tour is surviving on a specialist setup, Derven Acos is where the wreck budget runs out.

## The honest reading of the reviews

Steam shows **6,378 positive of 7,378 reviews (Very Positive, 86%)** on 17 October 2026, at **$59.99** with **49 achievements**. The complaints that recur are about contact and AI pressure rather than the circuits — which is the same thing as saying the wreck budget, not the lap time, is the difficulty.
