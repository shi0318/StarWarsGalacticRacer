## The four levers that will decide your build

Almost every arcade racer balances the same four levers. Expect Galactic Racer to expose some version of these:

- **Top speed** — your ceiling on straights. High-value on open circuits.
- **Acceleration** — how fast you recover after a crash, a corner, or a takedown. King on twisty tracks.
- **Handling / grip** — how tightly you can hold a line without sliding wide.
- **Boost** — how much overdrive you carry and how fast it recharges.

📖 In most combat racers, tuning trades these against **durability** — and in a game built around slamming rivals, durability matters more than usual. Push speed too hard and a single takedown can end your run. We expect a similar risk/reward layer here, but that is inference, not confirmation.

## A safe starter build (beginner-friendly)

If the game ships with tunable components, this is the setup we'd test first:

1. **Handling: high.** New players lose far more time to wall contact than to a low top speed. Prioritise grip until the track is memorised.
2. **Acceleration: medium-high.** Gets you back to pace quickly after mistakes or a rival's shunt.
3. **Top speed: medium.** Enough to stay competitive without overshooting corners.
4. **Boost: balanced recharge.** Favour a build that refills boost steadily over one giant one-shot burst.

This trades raw pace for consistency — the right call while you learn each circuit and how combat interrupts your rhythm.

## An advanced build (once you know the track)

After you've learned a circuit and stopped hitting walls, shift the sliders:

- Push **top speed** and **boost capacity** up
- Pull **handling** down only as far as you can still hit your lines
- Watch for any **durability** stat — in a combat racer, an over-tuned build that gets taken down on the final lap is slower than a stable one that finishes
