For your first **Star Wars: Galactic Racer Photo Mode** session, choose a short solo event you can repeat. Pick a landmark, decide where the craft should be relative to it, and use the Photo Mode controls shown in your current build. Secret Mode’s original announcement confirms the in-game mode and makes clear that a race scene was optional for its photography contest. That October event has closed. You can use the same framing ideas for your own collection. [Secret Mode’s Photo Mode announcement](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/4078430/view/1846651706677642)

## Start in a session you can repeat

A short Arcade event is a good way to capture a craft passing the same stretch of track. The publisher lists Arcade, Scenarios and the solo campaign as offline options after PC’s initial authentication. PlayStation lists pausing during offline gameplay and cinematics. Those details make a solo session a sensible first place to look for the Photo Mode control. They do not establish that every screen or event offers it. Check the control hints or available pause menu on your current platform before relying on the mode at a specific moment. [Publisher FAQ](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4078430/discussions/0/757303296993155447/) · [PlayStation game listing](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/star-wars-galactic-racer/)

Use a familiar route where you can recognize what comes before the landmark. If you are still learning the racing line, make one ordinary run before aiming for the image. Decide whether you want the approach, the moment your craft is level with the subject, or the exit. Starting with a planned place to check the camera is easier than trying to react to every interesting background. Use the [Arcade practice guide](/tips/galactic-racer-arcade-practice-guide/) to choose an event with a recognizable approach.

Keep your multiplayer and campaign objectives in mind. If the current mode does not let you pause, complete the event and test a similar scene offline. If a campaign event matters to your current Tour, start the photography session from a mode you are happy to repeat. The [mode guide](/game-modes/) explains which activities are available as alternatives.

## Choose what the image should show

- **The vehicle.** Put a single craft against a quieter area of the background. Check whether the engine light or reflection makes its profile easier to read. If the livery is the subject, leave enough room around the nose, tail and markings to see what you changed.
- **The place.** Select one visible landmark such as an ice gate, a desert wreck or the river beside the volcanic circuit. Keep the racer small enough to establish scale. Compare your reference with the [track and planet guide](/tracks/all-named-tracks/) if you remember the scenery and need an event name.
- **The moment.** For a jump, one useful comparison is the departure from the ramp versus the vehicle above the landing. For an overtake, compare a frame where the two craft still have visible space between them with one where they almost overlap. Good subject separation often makes the action easier to read than adding a stronger effect.
- **A scene away from racing.** The October announcement explicitly allowed photographs outside a race. If your chosen scene exposes Photo Mode, try framing the workshop, scenery or vehicle details. Inspect the mode where you want to shoot before planning around a particular menu, cinematic or vehicle bay.

## Work through one framing change at a time

1. **Make the first image wide.** Include the vehicle, landmark and enough track to explain the scene. On a curved route, give the craft room in the direction it is facing. On a straight, use the track edge to guide attention toward it.
2. **Make the second image tighter.** Keep the same subject and move in, or crop a copy later. If a bright background starts hiding the subject, change the angle slightly and compare the outline again.
3. **Try one change in height or angle.** A low viewpoint can give a craft more presence. A higher viewpoint can reveal a fork, bend or landing that explains the action. Use the camera controls actually available on your build. Where it gives you a display toggle, compare the original frame and the cleaner version before saving.
4. **Review both images at normal size.** Check the race craft at the edge of the frame, fine detail in bright effects and whether a rival, particle effect or piece of track furniture covers the subject. A shot that looks clear zoomed in can become hard to parse as a thumbnail.

Start simple if your view feels crowded. Reframe with one craft and one environmental clue before trying to reproduce an exact shot from an official trailer. This is especially useful on a busy track or when particle effects break up the silhouette. If the photo looks washed out only after export, compare the saved file and the image still on screen before altering the scene or your game display settings.

## Save a file you can actually find

Check the on-screen capture action, then confirm the shot is available in the platform’s media viewer or the storage location used by your capture tool. On PS5, Sony documents screenshot capture and access through the console’s Create controls and Media Gallery. A platform screenshot of the framed scene is also an option if the game exposes a usable view and you cannot find a dedicated save action. [PlayStation capture instructions](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/support/games/ps5-gameplay-screenshots-and-video-capture/)

Open the first saved shot before leaving the event. Make sure it shows the frame rather than a pause menu, and compare the saved colors with what you remember from the game. Keep the original if you plan to crop it. For a later recreation, note the track, mode, vehicle and the landmark you used to enter the frame. Two matching notes make it easy to compare the next attempt without having to reproduce a whole Tour.

Treat its images as examples of what the organizers encouraged, and look for a new publisher announcement before following any old submission instructions.
