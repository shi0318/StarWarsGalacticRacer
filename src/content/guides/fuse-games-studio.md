*Star Wars: Galactic Racer* is **Fuse Games' first game** — which makes "who are these people?" a fair question to ask before pre-ordering. The short answer: a **Guildford, UK** studio founded by five people out of Electronic Arts and Criterion, whose CV runs through **Burnout**, **Need for Speed** and **FIFA**. This page is built from the official interview with co-founder and CEO **Matt Webster**, published to the game's own Steam news hub on **3 July 2026**.

## Matt Webster: Vic-20 to Fuse Games

Webster's route in was, in his own words, accidental. He started on an older brother's **Commodore Vic-20** at nine or ten, moved through the **Commodore 64**, **Sony MSX** and **Atari ST**, and got hired straight out of A-Levels when a friend of his brother mentioned that "some American game company is coming to town and are hiring."

That company was **Electronic Arts**, and the job was in **Customer Service**. A week later he was in. Highlights of what followed, all from the interview:

| Era | What he worked on |
|---|---|
| Early career at EA | Moved from Customer Service into development; worked with **Bullfrog Studios** on many titles |
| Early 1990s / 16-bit era | Worked with EA's emerging internal teams; **created the initial design for EA Soccer** |
| Vancouver, Canada | With a small team, built the **first version of FIFA Soccer** |
| Later | The **Burnout** series — where "the real power of game 'feel' struck me" |

He spent **32 years at Electronic Arts** in total before founding Fuse Games with **four co-founders**.

## The studio's actual thesis: "game feel"

The most useful part of the interview is not the CV, it is the design philosophy — because it tells you what kind of racer this is going to be.

Webster's framing is that games are an interactive medium, so **the quality of the interactions is the product**. For a racing game specifically, he lays out four requirements:

1. Players must **believe they can expertly handle a vehicle at very high speed, at the edge of control**.
2. It has to be **approachable** — most people should get an exciting speed fantasy immediately.
3. It needs **accessible depth** that rewards striving for mastery.
4. **Risk and reward must feel compelling**, with meaningful, consequential decisions and visible improvement each run.

The studio name is a statement of the same idea: Fuse was founded to "fuse" attention to game feel with innovative play and strong technical and audio-visual execution. Webster also says the aim was a **modern, high-performing team combining deep experience with fresh talent** — "a studio that's built to last."

## How the Star Wars partnership happened

Fuse had **worked with Lucasfilm Games before**, so an early idea for the studio's first game was simply to combine their love of racing with their love of *Star Wars*. Webster's account: the more they thought about it, the more they fell for it. They pitched it to **Lucasfilm Games**, and that turned into the creative collaboration that became *Galactic Racer*, with the partnership running across the whole development period.

He is direct about the weight of it — being the team building a new *Star Wars* racing game "after such a long time" is described as both exciting and humbling.

## What the fantasy is meant to be

Webster's summary of the player fantasy: **be an elite racing pilot in the Star Wars galaxy**, wowed by the speed, immersed in an emerging racing series. What he wants to see from players is **build creativity** — learning how vehicles handle, how parts, upgrades and abilities combine, and pitting those builds against environments **across the Outer Rim and beyond**, earning rewards from risks taken.

## What Creative Director Kieran Crimmins has said

Fuse's Creative Director **Kieran Crimmins**, ex-**Criterion Games**, gave the on-record quotes during Summer Game Fest hands-on sessions. Two are worth knowing because they set expectations:

- On whether contact racing is viable, the way it was in Burnout: **"It's okay to give people a bit of a smash."** He adds that he doesn't think the studio would "ever really want to make an arcade racer without some of that fun in it."
- On build variety, he described the possible combinations as being **"in the trillions."**

Note the framing the team keeps insisting on: this is **not** a sequel to 1999's *Episode I: Racer*, and they would rather you didn't call it "Podracer." It covers **multiple types of racing** in the *Star Wars* universe — even though podracing's most iconic characters and courses are in it.

## Confirmed vs not confirmed

- **Confirmed (official interview):** Fuse Games was founded by Webster plus four co-founders; Webster spent 32 years at EA; he created the initial design for EA Soccer and helped build the first FIFA in Vancouver; he worked with Bullfrog and on Burnout; Fuse had worked with Lucasfilm Games before *Galactic Racer*; the studio's design priority is game feel and risk/reward.
- **On the record from the developer, via press:** the "bit of a smash" contact-racing stance and the "trillions" of build combinations, both from Crimmins.
- **Not confirmed:** studio headcount, the other four co-founders' names, and whether Fuse has a second project. The interview does not say.
